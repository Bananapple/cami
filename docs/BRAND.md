# Cami — Brand Foundations

A working summary of the branding decisions made so far. This is a living document — expect it to evolve as the product and audience sharpen.

---

## The product, in one line

Cami is studio operations software for boutique pilates and yoga studios — the calm, capable tool that handles bookings, member experience, payments, and websites, so studio owners can focus on running their practice.

---

## Ideal customer profile (ICP)

The buyer is the **owner-operator of a boutique pilates or yoga studio** — typically a single location or a small chain (1–5 studios), aesthetically sophisticated, design-forward, and underserved by the legacy incumbents (Mindbody, ClassPass-for-studios, etc.).

What's true about them:

- They care deeply about customer experience and the texture of their brand. Their studio is a curated space, and the software they use should feel like an extension of that — not a clunky 2010-era dashboard.
- They have substantial content already (Instagram, photography, member voice) but are stuck on tools that make presenting it well difficult.
- They are skeptical of corporate-feeling SaaS. Founder voice, taste, and restraint matter to them — they buy from brands they trust aesthetically.
- They are exhausted. Running a boutique studio means wearing many hats. The promise that resonates is *relief*, not *features*.

The buyer is **not** the end member/student. The brand should serve the operator, not the practitioner.

## Naming — why "Cami"

### The journey, briefly

The original name was **Lotus**, which was discarded for two reasons: (1) it's the dominant cliché in yoga/wellness branding (every studio has moved past it; it would make Cami feel like a previous generation of software), and (2) the name is extremely crowded across wellness, software, and consumer goods, making domain, trademark, and SEO defensibility prohibitively hard.

A wider exploration tested:

- **Monkfruit** — clever, but reads as DTC food/sweetener, not software.
- **ModernStudio / StudioOS** — descriptive category names, not brand names.
- **Savasana** — beautiful word, but means "corpse pose" (death of activity); wrong feeling for an operations tool.
- **Tabletop** — pilates pose + workspace double meaning, real contender, but slightly more pilates than yoga.
- **Solis** — pretty but generic; "Sol" wellness names are saturated.
- **Respire** — strong contender (means *breathe* in French), evocative and operator-focused, but ultimately founder felt cold on it.
- **Aesol** — generated by inversion-from-constraint; weak origin story.

Then the lane shifted from *editorial-quiet* (Solis, Respire) to *playful-distinctive* (Notion/Linear/Loom-era SaaS). In that lane, candidates included Posie, Hum, Kindly, Mira, Ode, Petal, Joli, Foli — and **Cami** emerged as the strongest.

### Why Cami works

1. **Person-name energy.** Cami reads as a soft, warm, friendly name — not a corporate product. This matters in a category where the incumbents feel cold and bureaucratic. "Cami handles your bookings" lands differently from "Mindbody handles your bookings."
2. **The hidden *ami* root.** *Ami* is French for *friend*. The brand thesis falls out of the name itself: the studio software that has your back. This is a layered meaning that rewards repeat exposure without requiring explanation.
3. **Memorability and brandability.** Two syllables, soft consonants, no hard sounds, easy to spell, easy to say. Survives word-of-mouth referrals.
4. **Serves both yoga and pilates audiences equally.** Unlike Tabletop (pilates-leaning) or Savasana (yoga-only), Cami doesn't favor either practice.
5. **Operator-feeling, not practice-feeling.** The best B2B names in adjacent spaces (Linear, Stripe, Notion, Ramp) don't evoke the end activity — they evoke *ease and craft for the operator*. Cami fits this pattern.

### Validation

- **Domain:** `heycami.studio` chosen as primary. The `.studio` TLD is on-brief (literally signals the audience). The "hey" prefix reinforces the friend/greeting positioning. `hicami.studio` and `withcami.com` recommended as defensive holds.
- **Trademark:** USPTO search across classes 9 (software) and 42 (cloud SaaS) returned no conflicts. Twelve live "Cami" marks exist across unrelated industries (music, health, lace, restaurants, museums, electrical hardware). The crowded-but-unrelated landscape works *in favor* — USPTO grants Cami marks freely because no one has a monopoly. Filing in classes 9 and 42 for studio software should be straightforward when commercial use begins.
- **Common law:** No Cami studio software, booking app, or wellness SaaS found in App Store, Product Hunt, Crunchbase, or general search.

## Brand tone and voice

### The big idea

**Cami is the friend who has your back.** Calm, capable, quietly confident. Not loud, not corporate, not clinical. The opposite of Mindbody.

### Voice principles

- **Warm but not precious.** Cami speaks like a thoughtful colleague, not a wellness influencer or a meditation app.
- **Confident through restraint.** Short sentences. No hyperbole. No exclamation points (mostly). The product does the talking.
- **Specific over abstract.** "Take payments without thinking about it" beats "delightful payment experiences."
- **Operator's language, not practitioner's.** Talk about bookings, no-shows, member retention, payouts — not chakras, intentions, or alignment.
- **First-person occasionally allowed.** Cami can occasionally speak as itself ("Hey. I'm Cami.") — used sparingly, this reinforces the friend-like personality without becoming twee.

### Hero copy

The provisional homepage line is:

> **Studio software that has your back.**

This pays off the name's hidden *ami* meaning, leads with the operator's relief (the felt benefit, not a feature), and is short enough to be confident. Alternative explored: *"Studio software, on your side."*

The earlier draft — "Your classes are beautiful, why shouldn't your guest experience be too?" — was rejected because rhetorical questions in hero positions invite shrugs and convert worse than declaratives.

## Visual direction

### Aesthetic positioning

The brand sits in the lane of **warm-modern boutique software**: soft, editorial, restrained, with deliberate personality. Reference points include Aesop (restraint), Solis (mood), and Notion-era SaaS (warmth + character). Crucially, it should *not* drift into pure wellness-editorial territory — the product must read as software, not a candle brand.

### Color

The hero color identified is a **warm ochre/mustard** (approximately `#BA7517`), drawn from the existing dashboard mockups. This pairs with:

- **Warm cream backgrounds** (`#F5EFE6`, `#FAF7F1`) for hero sections and surfaces.
- **Soft sand/clay tones** (`#EBE3D3`, `#EFE9DD`) for secondary surfaces.
- **Warm dark text** (`#2C2C2A` or `#1F1B16`) — never pure black; pure black is jarring against warm creams.

A second accent — likely a soft sage, terracotta, or deep forest green — should be introduced for variety in marketing assets, but the ochre/cream foundation is the spine.

### Typography

**Wordmark: Recoleta SemiBold, lowercase.**

Selected after exploring Migra Italic, Cabinet Grotesk, Tobias, Reckless Neue, Fraunces, Neco, Boxing, Pacifico, and the Recoleta family across all weights. Recoleta SemiBold won because:

- The chunky soft-serif strokes give it real presence without tipping into novelty.
- The rounded terminals are warm without being precious.
- It holds up at favicon size — the strokes are thick enough to survive scaling.
- Lowercase reinforces the boutique convention (Aesop, Solis-style restraint) and surfaces the *ami* visually.
- It's the family used by Wealthsimple, Lemonade, and many premium DTC/fintech brands — recognized as quality.

Treatment notes:
- Slight negative letter-spacing (`-0.02em` to `-0.03em`) so *cami* reads as a single visual unit.
- Color: warm dark, not pure black.
- Recoleta is commercial — license via Latinotype (~$50 single weight) or bundled in Adobe Fonts. **Fraunces** (free, Google Fonts) is the closest free alternative if needed pre-launch.

**Body and UI: paired humanist sans.** Inter (free), General Sans (free), or Söhne / GT America (paid) recommended for body text, dashboard labels, and small UI elements. Display type and UI type should be different — Recoleta is for the wordmark, headlines, and major brand moments; not for dense interfaces.

### Logo

**No symbol or icon — wordmark only.** The Recoleta SemiBold *cami* is distinctive enough to function alone at every scale, including favicon. Avoid the genre traps: lotus flowers, person-in-pose silhouettes, infinity loops, leaf marks, sun symbols. The font *is* the brand.

If a standalone mark is ever needed (app icon variations, social avatars), the lowercase *c* in Recoleta SemiBold has enough character to do the work without a separate symbol.

## Studio-manager imagery and product mockup language

The dashboard and product mockup imagery established so far includes:

- A **booking card** ("Cycling, 10:00–11:00 AM, 12 spots left, Intermediate") with a warm ochre tag for difficulty/category — this color accent is a key brand carrier.
- An **upcoming class card** with member avatar and "Add a guest" CTA.
- A **weekly overview chart** using soft teal/mint tones (waitlist, confirmed, cancelled segmentation), paired with first-time/returning customer metrics. The teal here functions as a quiet secondary palette element.
- An **iOS notification stack** ("Ready for your next class?", "February special, just for you.", "Time to breathe. Restore is live.") on a softly-blurred warm-textured background — this is product-as-feeling, not product-as-feature.

Together these establish a visual language where:

- Product UI lives on **white or warm-cream surfaces** with generous whitespace.
- **Soft drop shadows or floating-card treatments** are acceptable for marketing mockups, but the dashboard itself should feel flat and calm.
- **The ochre accent is reserved for emphasis** (difficulty tags, primary CTAs, key data points) — used sparingly so it retains weight.
- **People are present but secondary.** Imagery of studio members or instructors should appear softly blurred, partially in frame, or as supporting context — never the hero of a layout. The hero is the product, the typography, and the warm space around them.

## Homepage structure (working)

A provisional homepage outline, to be refined:

1. **Hero** — wordmark top-left, generous whitespace, hero line ("Studio software that has your back."), primary CTA + quieter secondary CTA.
2. **Product proof** — booking card and dashboard mockups against warm cream surfaces.
3. **The "Cami has your back" story** — three to five capabilities (booking, member experience, payments, website, communication), illustrated calmly.
4. **Communication/notifications section** — the iOS notification mockup, captioned around how Cami handles member touchpoints with care.
5. **Pilot studio logos** — even three are enough at launch.
6. **Founder note** — short paragraph from the founder on why Cami exists. Boutique buyers respond to founder voice; this is a competitive advantage over Mindbody.
7. **CTA repeat** — same primary CTA, restated calmly.
8. **Footer** — quiet, organized.

Live notification pop-ups in the hero were considered and **deferred** — they distract from the main message and feel performative on a brand built around calm.
